\begin{abstract}
Dark matter distribution around galaxy pairs encodes information about the gravitational interaction between their host halos and probes the associated filamentary structure. In this work, we use the Sloan Digital Sky Survey III (SDSS III) Baryonic Oscillation Spectroscopic Survey (BOSS) CMASS galaxy sample and the Planck cosmic microwave background (CMB) lensing map to study the matter distribution around galaxy pairs. We produce stacked CMB lensing $\kappa$ maps around galaxy pairs, in transverse pair separation bins of $4$--$6$, $6$--$15$, and $15$--$25\,\hinvMpc$. With comparison to the maps from control pairs, obtained by superposition of $\kappa$ distribution around single galaxies, a quadrupole pattern in the matter distribution around galaxy pairs is revealed for the first time, with excess around the pair connection line and deficit around the perpendicular bisector. Such a pattern is consistent with the expectation from cosmological $N$-body simulations, pointing to an origin from tidal interaction. There is a hint of matter excess in between galaxy pairs, associated with filaments, at the $2.4\sigma$ level for pairs with transverse separation of $6$--$15\,\hinvMpc$, and an increased galaxy pair sample and/or CMB lensing reconstruction with improved precision would be necessary for a firm detection.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\label{sec:intro}

In the standard model of cosmic structure formation, gravitational instability drives the primordial density fluctuations to grow into a cosmic web structure \citep[e.g.,][]{Zeldovich1970, BondKofmanPogosyan1996}. Galaxies, which form and evolve within dark matter halos \citep[e.g.,][]{WhiteRees1978}, provide a luminous tracer of the underlying dark matter distribution. As dark matter halos are mainly located at the nodes of the cosmic web structure, galaxies need to be combined with probes sensitive to matter distribution to effectively explore the cosmic web structure to test cosmic structure formation. In this paper, we study the matter distribution around galaxy pairs by combining galaxy survey data with Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) lensing data.

In previous work, the technique of weak gravitational lensing has been applied to study the filament between galaxy pairs. 
\citet{Clampitt2016} measure the stacked shear signal around pairs of SDSS luminous red galaxies (LRGs) at $z\approx0.25$ with projected separations of $6$--$14\,\hinvMpc$ and line-of-sight separations below $6\,\hinvMpc$, with SDSS source galaxies for shape measurements covering approximately $8000\,\mathrm{deg}^{2}$. 
They devise a four-point shear estimator based on symmetry to
cancel the signal of two spherical halos and isolate the signal of bridge (filament) between galaxy pairs. They report a $4.5\sigma$ detection of filament between galaxy pairs. Based on 154 deg$^2$ CFHTLenS lensing data, \citet{EppsHudson2017} stack the weak-lensing signal around BOSS LOWZ and CMASS pairs with projected physical separation of $6$--$10\,\hinvMpc$ at a mean redshift
$\langle z\rangle\approx0.42$. They used pairs with the same projected separation but a larger line-of-sight separation as a control, and reported a $5\sigma$ excess after subtracting this control from the physical-pair stack. Combining three lensing surveys (KV450, RCSLenS, and CFHTLenS), \citet{Xia2020} do not detect the filament signal seen in \citet{EppsHudson2017}. Instead, they adopt the nulling method of \citet{Clampitt2016} and report a 3.4$\sigma$ detection of filaments between pairs of BOSS LRGs with projected separation of $3$--$5\,\hinvMpc$. Similarly, with $z\sim 0.55$ BOSS CMASS galaxies and the first-year Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam shape catalog ($\sim 140\,\mathrm{deg}^{2}$ overlap region with $\sim$14k CMASS galaxies), \citet{Kondo2020} have a 3.9$\sigma$ detection of the filaments between galaxy pairs of projected physical separation of $6$--$14\,\hinvMpc$.

The CMB provides a complementary lensing source. Instead of the shear signal in galaxy weak lensing, CMB lensing traces projected mass distribution, or the convergence. For a spatially flat universe with sources at comoving distance $\chi_{\rm s}$, the convergence in direction $\hat{\boldsymbol n}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(\hat{\boldsymbol n}) = \int_{0}^{\chi_{\rm s}} \mathrm{d}\chi\,
  W(\chi)\,\delta(\chi\hat{\boldsymbol n},\chi),
  \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
with the kernel  
\begin{equation}  
W(\chi) = \frac{3\Omega_{\rm m}H_{0}^{2}}{2c^{2}}\,
  \frac{\chi}{a(\chi)}\,\frac{\chi_{\rm s}-\chi}{\chi_{\rm s}},
  \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
where $\chi$ is comoving distance corresponding to scale factor $a$ 
\citep{Bartelmann2001}. For CMB lensing, the source is at the last scattering surface $z_s\sim 1090$, and the kernel $W(\chi)$ is broad and peaks near $z\sim2$ \citep{LewisChallinor2006}. 
Compared with shear-based weak lensing, the CMB lensing reconstruction is free of galaxy shape noise and intrinsic alignments. Two earlier studies use filaments identified directly from the spectroscopic galaxy distribution. \citet{He2018} apply a density-ridge algorithm to the CMASS galaxy distribution
to construct a filament intensity map. Cross-correlating the map with the Planck $\kappa$ map \citep{Planck2018lensing} leads to a $5\sigma$ detection of the correlation and a filament bias $b_{\rm f}\approx1.5$. \citet{Tanimura2020} identify $\sim$ 25k filaments of length $30$--$100\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ in the LOWZ+CMASS galaxy distribution with DisPerSE \citep{Sousbie2011}. Stacking the Planck $\kappa$ map on the filament spines, they reach an $8.1\sigma$ detection of the correlation. 

While focusing on studying the thermal Sunyaev–Zel’dovich signal \citep{SZ1980} of filament gas, \citet{deGraaff2019} also show a result of mean convergence around galaxy pairs. They stack Planck $\kappa$ map (with $10$-arcmin smoothing) around $10^6$ pairs of BOSS CMASS galaxies, with transverse comoving separations of $6$--$14\,\hinvMpc$ and line-of-sight separations below $5\,\hinvMpc$. Each pair is rotated to a common orientation, rescaled to unit separation, projected onto a rectangular grid, and reflected to make the map symmetric. Isotropic halo contributions are removed by fitting an isotropic radial profile within a $60^{\circ}$ cone around one galaxy and perpendicular to the pair axis. They obtain a $1.9\sigma$ measurement of the mean convergence of filament between galaxy pair. The significance increases to $3.1\sigma$ if the region beyond the pair is included.

In this paper, we combine the Planck CMB lensing measurements with the CMASS galaxy sample to carry out a detailed study of the matter distribution around galaxy pairs. Together with a control map based on single galaxies,  we investigate the matter distribution as a function of pair separation and compare the measurements with those from a cosmological $N$-body simulation. The paper is organized as follows. In Section~\ref{sec:data}, we introduce the galaxy sample, the CMB lensing data, and the cosmological $N$-body simulation. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the matter distribution measurements from stacking the CMB $\kappa$ map and the corresponding calculations with the mock catalog constructed based on the $N$-body simulation. In Section~\ref{sec:sim_expect}, we present the $\Lambda$CDM reference signal from the mock catalog. In Section~\ref{sec:results}, we analyze the observational measurements and make comparisons to the model prediction from the mock catalog. Finally, in Section~\ref{sec:discussion} we summarize the results and discuss their implications.

Throughout the paper we adopt the Planck 2018 cosmology \citep{Planck2020} and quote comoving distances in $\hinvMpc$ unless otherwise stated, where $h=0.6766$ is the Hubble constant $H_0$ in units of $100\, {\rm km\, s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}$.


\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

In this work of studying the matter distribution around galaxy pairs, we combine three ingredients: a spectroscopic galaxy sample, which defines galaxy pairs and single-galaxy control; CMB lensing data, which is used to reveal the matter distribution around galaxies; and a mock galaxy catalog based on an $N$-body cosmological simulation, which provides a $\Lambda$CDM reference model to guide observational measurements and aids interpretation.

\subsection{BOSS CMASS galaxy sample}
\label{sec:data_cmass}

\begin{figure*}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth, trim=0 -3cm 0 0]{footprint.pdf}
  \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{zdist.pdf}
  \caption{Left: footprint of the BOSS CMASS North and South samples in
  Galactic coordinates, overlaid on the Planck 2018 lensing analysis mask.
  Right: weighted redshift distribution of the CMASS galaxies in each Galactic cap
  and in total, with the $0.4<z<0.7$ selection shaded and the median
  redshift of the selected sample marked.}
  \label{fig:footprint_zdist}
\end{figure*}


In our study, galaxy positions come from the CMASS sample of the Baryon Oscillation
Spectroscopic Survey \citep[BOSS;][]{Dawson2013}, part of SDSS-III 
\citep{Eisenstein2011}, in its twelfth data release
\citep{Alam2015, Reid2016}. CMASS galaxies are LRGs, which are selected with color and
magnitude cuts designed to follow a passively evolving stellar-population
locus so that the sample has an approximately constant stellar mass across
its redshift range (hence the name). They are
hosted by halos of typical mass $M\sim 10^{13}\,\hinvMsun$,
with a satellite fraction of about 10 percent \citep{White2011}. 
Simulations predict that pairs of such halos are often connected by filaments \citep{Colberg2005}.

We use data from both Galactic caps, referred to as North and South, and restrict the sample to $0.4<z<0.7$. After this cut the sample contains $792{,}294$ galaxies, $579{,}089$ in the
North and $213{,}205$ in the South. The effective survey area is approximately $6{,}850\,\mathrm{deg}^{2}$ in the North and $2{,}525\,\mathrm{deg}^{2}$ in the South, for a total of
$9{,}376\,\mathrm{deg}^{2}$ \citep{Reid2016}. The left panel of Figure~\ref{fig:footprint_zdist} shows the footprint of both caps together with the Planck lensing mask. The sample has a median redshift of $z=0.539$, with galaxies in the two caps having similar redshift distributions (right panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:footprint_zdist}).

Each CMASS galaxy carries the standard BOSS systematic weights 
\citep{Ross2012, Reid2016}. We combine them as
\begin{equation}
  w = w_{\rm seeing}\, w_{\rm star}\,
      \left(w_{\rm noz} + w_{\rm cp} - 1\right),
  \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
where $w_{\rm seeing}$ and $w_{\rm star}$ correct the angular selection
function for variations in seeing and stellar density, $w_{\rm cp}$ accounts
for close pairs lost to fiber collisions, and $w_{\rm noz}$ accounts for
redshift failures. We do not apply the FKP weight because it is designed to
optimize clustering measurements rather than the stacking estimator in this work.

In addition to the galaxy catalog, we also use the BOSS random catalog \citep{Reid2016}, which samples the angular selection function of each cap at roughly fifty times the galaxy density, with redshifts assigned from the observed galaxy distribution. After applying the
same $0.4<z<0.7$ cut, we end up with $40.9$ million points over both Galactic caps. 
As shown later, the random catalog will be used for corrections related to the residual 
mean-field or mask-related offset in the CMB $\kappa$ map and for constructing the jackknife regions for uncertainty estimation.

\subsection{Planck CMB lensing data}
\label{sec:data_planck}

We use the Planck 2018 lensing reconstruction \citep{Planck2018lensing},
release \texttt{COM\_Lensing\_4096\_R3.00}. The convergence harmonic
coefficients $\kappa_{LM}$ are taken from the minimum-variance combination
of the temperature and polarization quadratic estimators applied to the
SMICA foreground-cleaned maps. The estimated mean field has been subtracted, and the coefficients are provided up to $L_{\rm max}=4096$. 

We produce a HEALPix \citep{Gorski2005} $\kappa$ map with $N_{\rm side}=2048$, corresponding to a pixel scale of $1.7\,\mathrm{arcmin}$, smoothed with a Gaussian beam of $8\,\mathrm{arcmin}$ full width at half maximum (FWHM). The smoothing suppresses small-scale reconstruction noise. At $z=0.55$, $8\,\mathrm{arcmin}$ corresponds to $\sim 3.3\,\hinvMpc$ (comoving). 

We apply the Planck lensing analysis mask distributed with the release,
which removes the Galactic plane, point sources, and regions of poor
foreground cleaning and retains 67.1 percent of the sky. 
The CMASS footprint lies almost entirely within the unmasked region: 
96.5 percent of North and 94.7 percent of South galaxies, 96.0 percent overall, 
fall on unmasked pixels (Fig.~\ref{fig:footprint_zdist}). 

\subsection{$N$-body simulation and mock galaxy catalog}
\label{sec:sim}

For a $\Lambda$CDM reference model of the matter distribution around galaxy pairs, 
we build a mock galaxy catalog from BigMDPL, an $N$-body cosmological simulation 
in the MultiDark suite \citep{Klypin2016, Riebe2013}. BigMDPL follows $3840^{3}$ 
dark-matter particles of mass $2.36\times10^{10}\,\hinvMsun$ in a periodic
box of comoving side $L=2500\,\hinvMpc$, adopting a cosmology with $\Omega_m=0.307$ and $h=0.678$. We work with the snapshot at $a=0.6464$ ($z=0.547$) to closely match the 
mean redshift of the CMASS galaxy sample. We retain parent halos in the public halo catalog, with halos identified with the Rockstar algorithm \citep{behroozi2012rockstar} and  adopt the virial mass as the halo mass. 

We assign galaxies to halos above $\log_{10}[M/(\hinvMsun)]=12.5$ (hereafter halo mass is in units of $\hinvMsun$) within the halo occupation distribution (HOD) framework. The mean occupation function of central galaxies follows the form in \citet{White2011} for CMASS galaxies,
\begin{equation}
  \langle N_{\rm cen}(M)\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\left[1+\mathrm{erf}
  \left(\frac{\ln(M/M_{\rm cut})}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\right)\right],
  \label{eq:hod}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma=0.94$. Given the low satellite fraction ($\sim 10\%$; \citealt{White2011}), 
we only assign central galaxies by setting the cutoff mass scale $\log_{10}M_{\rm cut}=13.246$ to match the CMASS central-galaxy number density, $2.70\times10^{-4}\,h^{3}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$.

We choose one principal axis of the simulation box to be the line-of-sight direction to put the mock galaxy catalog in redshift space. That is, the line-of-sight position of each galaxy is shifted by $(1+z)\,v_{\parallel}/H(z)$, with $v_{\parallel}$ denoting the line-of-sight peculiar-velocity of the host halo and $H(z)$ being the Hubble parameter at redshift $z$ and with the periodic boundary condition accounted for. 

With the $N$-body simulation, we also construct a simulated convergence map from 
the 0.5 percent randomly sampled particles of the simulation, with $N_{\rm tot}=2.8\times10^{8}$ particles in total. We project the particles along the chosen 
principal axis of the simulation box onto a periodic 2-dimensional (2D) grid with 
pixel width $0.1\,\hinvMpc$. For each pixel, we calculate the mean-subtracted surface 
density as
\begin{equation}
    \Sigma(\mathbf{x})=m_{\rm eff}\left[\frac{N_{\rm p}(\mathbf{x})}{\ell^{2}}
-\frac{N_{\rm tot}}{L^{2}}\right],
\end{equation}
where $N_{\rm p}(\mathbf{x})$ is the number of particles in the pixel of width $\ell$, and $N_{\rm tot}$ is the total number of particles in the box of size $L$, and $m_{\rm eff}$ is the effective mass of each particle in the sub-sampled particle catalog (i.e., 200 times the particle mass in the simulation). We convert the surface density to convergence as $\kappa=\Sigma/\Sigma_{\rm crit}$, where the comoving critical density $\Sigma_{\rm crit}=8.88\times10^{14}\,h\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-2}$ corresponds to a lens at $z=0.547$ and the CMB as source.
Similar to the CMB lensing map, the simulation $\kappa$ map is smoothed with a periodic two-dimensional Gaussian kernel of FWHM $3.31\,\hinvMpc$, corresponding to $8\,\mathrm{arcmin}$ at the snapshot redshift.

With the mock galaxy catalog and the simulated $\kappa$ map, we are able to produce theoretical model predictions corresponding to the measurements based on the CMASS galaxy catalog and Planck CMB $\kappa$ map.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:method}

We select galaxy pairs from the CMASS galaxy catalog and stack the Planck CMB $\kappa$ map around pairs in the pair frame. We also extract certain bands from the stacked $\kappa$ map to form band statistics to characterize the matter distribution. A similar procedure is applied to mock galaxies and simulated $\kappa$ map. Uncertainties on the measurements are estimated by spatial jackknife over the survey footprint or by jackknife over 25
blocks of the simulation box.

\subsection{Convergence map of galaxy pairs}
\label{sec:pairs}

We construct samples of galaxy pairs according to the line-of-sight and transverse separations,
\begin{equation}
  r_{\parallel} = |D_{{\rm c},1}-D_{{\rm c},2}|, \qquad
  r_{\perp} = \theta_{12}\,\frac{D_{{\rm c},1}+D_{{\rm c},2}}{2},
  \label{eq:separations}
\end{equation}
where $D_{\rm c,1}$ and $D_{\rm c,2}$ are the comoving distances of the two galaxies and $\theta_{12}$ is their angular separation (in radians; for all galaxy pairs studied in this work we are in the small-angle regime, $\theta_{12}\ll1$).  Pairs are selected to satisfy $r_{\parallel}\leq r_{\parallel,{\rm max}}$ and
$r_{\perp,{\rm min}}\leq r_{\perp} < r_{\perp,{\rm max}}$.
We use three contiguous $r_\perp$ bins, $4$--$6$, $6$--$15$, and $15$--$25\,\hinvMpc$, with a line-of-sight cut of $r_{\parallel,{\rm max}}=5\,\hinvMpc$ for all three. The pair-weighted mean separations in the three bins are $5.0$, $10.7$, and $20.2\,\hinvMpc$, and we label them with the nominal separations $d=5$, $10$, and $20\,\hinvMpc$. Each pair is assigned the comoving distance $D_c$ at the mean redshift of its members. 

%\subsection{Stacking in the pair frame}
%\label{sec:stacking}

Each pair of galaxies defines a local Cartesian frame on the sky. The $X$ axis runs from
galaxy 1 to galaxy 2, with the pair ordered so that $X$ increases with
Galactic longitude, and the $Y$ axis is perpendicular to the pair axis. 
Note that we adopt the Galactic coordinate system, the native frame of the CMB maps. The $\kappa$ field around the pair is sampled with a $101\times101$ grid extending
to $\pm50\,\hinvMpc$ in both directions, with a spacing of
$1\,\hinvMpc$ and the central grid point at the pair midpoint.

In the small-angle approximation, a grid point $(X,Y)$ maps to Galactic coordinates $(l, b)$ according to
\begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix}  
    \Delta l \cos b_c \\  
    \Delta b \\  
  \end{pmatrix}  
=
  \begin{pmatrix}  
    \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\  
    \sin\phi &  \cos\phi \\
  \end{pmatrix}  
  \begin{pmatrix}  
    X/D_c\\
    Y/D_c\\
  \end{pmatrix},  
  \label{eq:grid_to_sky}
\end{equation}
where $(l_{\rm c},b_{\rm c})$ is the pair midpoint, $\Delta l = l-l_{\rm c}$, $\Delta b = b-b_{\rm c}$, and $\phi$ is the angle between the $X$ axis and the local latitude line, defined by
$(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)\propto(\Delta l\cos b_{\rm c},\Delta b)$.
Each grid point is assigned the CMB $\kappa$ value of the HEALPix pixel that contains its sky position. 

The stacked map is the weighted mean at each grid point,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\kappa_{\rm gp}(X,Y) = \frac{\sum_{i} w_{i}\,m_{i}(X,Y)\,\kappa_{i}(X,Y)}
                         {\sum_{i} w_{i}\,m_{i}(X,Y)},
  \label{eq:stack}
\end{equation}
where $i$ runs over pairs and $m_{i}\in\{0,1\}$ is the mask value at
the sampled HEALPix pixel. The pair weight is
\begin{equation}
  w_{i} = w_{1}\,w_{2}\,\Sigma_{\rm crit}^{-2}(\bar z_{i}),
  \label{eq:pair_weight}
\end{equation}
the product of the two galaxy weights of Equation~(\ref{eq:weights}) and a
lensing-efficiency weight evaluated at the mean redshift $\bar z_{i}$ of the pair \citep[e.g.,][]{EppsHudson2017}. Here
$\Sigma_{\rm crit}\propto D_{\rm A}(z_{\rm s})/[D_{\rm A}(z)\,D_{\rm A}(z,z_{\rm s})]$, 
where the angular diameter distances are evaluated at the lens redshift, the source redshift ($z_{\rm s}=1090$ for CMB as source), and between the lens and source redshift. This weight favors the redshifts at
which a given mass produces the largest convergence. 
Given that the two galaxies are statistically interchangeable and that there is no preferred handedness about the pair axis, we symmetrize  the stacked map by replacing the value at $(X,Y)$ with the mean of those at the four pixels $(\pm X,\pm Y)$.

Following the same procedure, we also obtain a stacked map $\hat\kappa_{\rm rp}(X,Y)$ for pairs of random points, drawn from the full random catalog with the same $r_{\perp}$ and $r_{\parallel}$ cuts and the same weighting as the galaxy pairs. The random-pair map absorbs the mean convergence of the footprint and any offset associated with the mask or the mean field of the reconstruction \citep[e.g.,][]{Singh2017}. Our final galaxy pair convergence map is 
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_{\rm p}(X,Y) 
    = \hat\kappa_{\rm gp}(X,Y) -\hat\kappa_{\rm rp}(X,Y).
\end{equation}

With the mock galaxies and the simulated $\kappa$ map, we follow a similar procedure to construct the stacked $\kappa$ map around mock galaxy pairs, following Equation~(\ref{eq:stack}) and without applying weights and mask. There is no need to produce a map from random pairs.

\subsection{Control pair map and band statistics}
\label{sec:control}

Because of the environment and gravitational interaction of galaxy pairs, the matter distribution around galaxy pairs deviates from that from the simple superposition of two single galaxies at the corresponding pair separation. A control pair $\kappa$ map can be constructed following the latter and its comparison with the true pair $\kappa$ map can help reveal the matter distribution and filament structure around galaxy pairs.

To construct a control map, we stack the CMB $\kappa$ map around individual galaxies on a $100\times100$ grid of $1\,\hinvMpc$ cells
centered on each galaxy, with the weight $w\,\Sigma_{\rm crit}^{-2}(z)$. The stacked map around random points is similarly obtained. The mean stacked $\kappa$ map around a single galaxy is the difference between the galaxy stack and random point stack. The map is also symmetrized by averaging pixels in annuli about the center.

With the radial profile $P(r)$ from the single galaxy stacked $\kappa$ map, we construct the control pair map on the same $101\times101$ $(X,Y)$ grid used for true galaxy pairs. For a pair of separation $r_{\perp}$, the superposition of the $\kappa$ distribution from two single galaxies is
\begin{eqnarray}
  T(X,Y;r_{\perp})  =& P\!\left(\sqrt{(X-r_{\perp}/2)^{2}+Y^{2}}\right)\nonumber\\
         & + P\!\left(\sqrt{(X+r_{\perp}/2)^{2}+Y^{2}}\right),
  \label{eq:two_halo}
\end{eqnarray}
To account for the distribution of transverse separation of galaxy pairs in each pair sample, we put galaxy pairs into fine $r_\perp$ separation bins of width $0.5\,\hinvMpc$. The control pair map is then computed as
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_{\rm ctl}(X,Y) = \frac{\sum_{b} c_{b}(X,Y)\,T(X,Y;r_{b})}
                               {\sum_{b} c_{b}(X,Y)},
  \label{eq:control}
\end{equation}
where $b$ runs over the fine $r_\perp$ bins, $r_{b}$ is the weighted mean $r_\perp$ separation of the pairs in fine bin $b$, and
$c_{b}(X,Y)=\sum_{i\in b} w_{i}\,m_{i}(X,Y)$ is the weight that these pairs
contribute to the control map. 
%The control therefore has the same mix of separations and the same per-pixel weighting as the pair stack of Equation~(\ref{eq:stack}), and receives the same fourfold reflection average. The mock map has no mask and the mock pairs are unweighted, so $c_{b}$ reduces to the number of pairs in the fine bin.
%This control has unit amplitude and no free parameters. 
With the true pair map and the control pair map, we take their difference to construct the residual map,
\begin{equation}
  F(X,Y) = \kappa_{\rm p}(X,Y)-\kappa_{\rm ctl}(X,Y).
  \label{eq:filament}
\end{equation}
%where gp and rp denote the galaxy-pair and random-pair stacks.
This residual map is used to study the matter distribution pattern around galaxy pairs and to search for signatures of filaments between the
paired galaxies. 
%We call it a residual map, rather than a filament map, because imperfect halo subtraction and differences between paired and average galaxy environments can also contribute.

%\subsection{Band statistic}
%\label{sec:bands}

To quantify the signal in the residual map, we form compressed 1D profiles along the $X$ direction, including a central band profile $\langle F(X,Y)\rangle_{\rm cen}$ and an off-center band profile $\langle F(X,Y)\rangle_{\rm off}$. The former is obtained by averaging the central rows with $|Y|\leq1.5\,\hinvMpc$, and the latter by averaging the rows with $1.5\leq|Y|\leq10.5\,\hinvMpc$. We also form a difference profile, 
\begin{equation}
    B(X)=\langle F(X,Y)\rangle_{\rm cen} - \langle F(X,Y)\rangle_{\rm off},
    \label{eq:band}
\end{equation}
which suppresses components that vary slowly with $Y$. We define the bridge excess as the mean of $B(X)$ over the interval $|X|\leq 0.35 d$, with $d$ the nominal pair separation.

As shown later, we fit the band profiles with full covariance matrices. To obtain reliable covariance matrix estimates, we reduce the dimension of band profiles by evaluating them in bins of width $4\,\hinvMpc$ (comparable to the smoothing scale of the CMB $\kappa$ map).  For covariance-based fits, we have the central-band profile in 11 $X>0$ bins, with centers ranging from $2\,\hinvMpc$ to $42\,\hinvMpc$; similarly for the off-center-band profile. The total dimension of the vector of central- and off-center-band profiles is then 22.

We also apply the same procedure (but without weights and mask) to produce control maps and band profiles with the mock galaxies and the simulated $\kappa$ map.

\subsection{Uncertainty and covariance estimates}
\label{sec:errors}

We estimate uncertainties on the observed maps and covariance matrices of band profiles with the jackknife method. 
The footprint is divided into $K=287$ contiguous regions of
approximately equal size, constructed from HEALPix pixels with
$N_{\rm side}=10$. 

For any statistic $\mathbf{x}$ (pixel value of a map or band profile), we measure it $K$ times by leaving out one jackknife region each time. The jackknife covariance is calculated as
\begin{equation}
  %\sigma^{2}=\frac{K-1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(x_{k}-\bar x)^{2}, \qquad
  \mathsf{C}=\frac{K-1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}})
                                          (\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}})^{\mathsf T},
  \label{eq:jackknife}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathbf{x}}$ is the mean of the leave-one-out values. The square roots of the diagonal elements are used as error bars in the relevant plots. 

To fit the amplitude $A$ of a fixed template vector $\mathbf{s}$ from the mock to a measurement data vector $\mathbf{d}$ from the observation, we minimize
\begin{equation}
  \chi^{2}(A) =(\mathbf{d}-A\mathbf{s})^{\mathsf T}\mathsf{C}^{-1}
                 (\mathbf{d}-A\mathbf{s}), 
\end{equation}
which has the solution and error bar
\begin{equation}
  \hat A =\frac{\mathbf{s}^{\mathsf T}\mathsf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{d}}
                 {\mathbf{s}^{\mathsf T}\mathsf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{s}}, \qquad
  \sigma_{A}=\left(\mathbf{s}^{\mathsf T}\mathsf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{s}\right)^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:amplitude}
\end{equation}

We first analyze the stacked $\kappa$ maps based on the mock galaxy catalog and the $\kappa$ map constructed from the $N$-body simulation, which provide a reference model to guide our observational measurements and analysis. 

Figure~\ref{fig:sim_pair_vs_control} shows the mock pair stack for pairs with transverse separations in the range of $6$--$15\,\hinvMpc$ (the $r_\perp \sim 10\,\hinvMpc$ pairs), along with its control constructed from the stack with single galaxies. The two maps are nearly indistinguishable on a common color scale because the difference is small relative to the signal around the galaxies. A close look reveals that the pair stack appears to be more squashed along the $Y$ direction than the control stack. Similar patterns are also found for the $r_\perp \sim 5\,\hinvMpc$ and $r_\perp \sim 20\,\hinvMpc$ pair samples.

Taking the difference of the two maps, we obtain the residual maps for the three $r_{\perp}$ bins, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:sim_residual_maps}. 
The residual maps all show a quadrupole pattern, with a negative signal close to the $Y$ axis and a positive signal (or less negative signal for pairs of smallest separations) close to the $X$ axis. Such a pattern is consistent with a tidal origin --- as a result of the tidal interaction of galaxy pairs, dark matter distribution tends to be stretched along the pair connecting line and to be squashed along the perpendicular direction. The ridge between the pair of galaxies is the filament-associated component. In the outskirts of the pair and close to the $X$ axis, the tidal effect causes the matter distribution to become stretched and extended. We note that near the position of each galaxy, the pixel value does not go to zero, as a result of both the tidal effect and pair environment effect. The overall amplitude of the quadrupole matter distribution decreases as the pair separation increases.

Figure~\ref{fig:sim_band_profiles} shows the corresponding band profiles. For pairs of separation $4$--$6\,\hinvMpc$ (left panel), the central band becomes negative, which is likely caused by the environment effect of close pairs.  The difference between the central and off-center band profile, however, is positive, which shows a bridge between pairs and a declining profile in the outskirts. For the $6$--$15\,\hinvMpc$ pairs (middle panel), the central band profile between the galaxies is weakly positive, and for the $15$--$25\,\hinvMpc$ pairs (right panel) it is clearly positive. In all three cases, the off-center band profile between the galaxies is negative. It dominates the difference profile for the $4$--$6$ and $6$--$15\,\hinvMpc$ pairs, while the central and off-center bands contribute comparably for the $15$--$25\,\hinvMpc$ pairs. Near the galaxy positions (indicated by the dotted vertical lines), the residual does not go to zero, as explained above. The bridge excess, calculated within $|X|\leq0.35\,d$ with $d$ the nominal separation, is $\left(5.97\pm0.35,\ 4.91\pm0.21,\ 2.18\pm0.11\right)\times10^{-4}$ for the $4$--$6$, $6$--$15$, and $15$--$25\,\hinvMpc$ pairs. The decline with separation is consistent with the expectation that the tidal effect and environment effect become less important for pairs at larger separation.

The bridge statistic is highly compressed, as it does not reflect the quadrupole pattern of the matter distribution around galaxy pairs. The central and off-center band profiles provide an improved description of the matter distribution. The data points in Figure~\ref{fig:band_cen_vs_off_obs_sim}  are measurements of these band profiles. Central band profiles are close to zero, as we average over three central rows in the observed residual maps, which dilutes the excess on the pair connection line. Off-center band profiles clearly show a deficit on scales $\lesssim 15\,\hinvMpc$. To see the overall significance of detecting the central and off-center band profiles, we perform a $\chi^{2}$ test of the compressed $22$-element central and off-center band profiles against zero with the full covariance, which gives $\chi^{2}=34.8$, $41.2$, and $49.9$ for $22$ degrees of freedom in the three $r_\perp$ bins (corresponding to nominal $p$ values of $p=0.04$, $0.008$, and $6\times10^{-4}$). These tests apply to the full profile, including bins that contain the galaxies. The $\chi^2$ values demonstrate that the matter distribution around true galaxy pairs is significantly different from that around the control pairs.

To see how well the central and off-center band profiles are described by the reference model from the mock, we fit the observed profiles with the mock residual profiles over the full
$22$-element vector using Equation~(\ref{eq:amplitude}), which gives

Overall, we find that the measured matter distribution around galaxy pairs is significantly different from that from simple superposition of those from single galaxies. Similar to that seen in the model based on mock galaxies built from the $N$-body simulation, the matter distribution shows a quadrupole pattern, consistent with a tidal origin. As a whole, by cross-correlating CMASS galaxy pairs with the Planck CMB lensing map, we detect the matter distribution around galaxy pairs in the three $r_{\perp}$ bins. Comparing to the control pair map, we find the expected quadrupole pattern in the residual matter distribution around galaxy pairs. There is a hint of stretched matter (filaments) along the pair connection line, while their significance needs to be further examined by increasing the size of the galaxy pair sample.

\section{Summary and Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

In this work, we use the BOSS CMASS galaxy sample and Planck CMB lensing map to probe the matter distribution (dominated by dark matter) around galaxy pairs with a stacking analysis. We have stacked the Planck 2018 lensing convergence map around $\sim 0.2$ million, $\sim 1.2$ million, and $\sim 1.6$ million BOSS CMASS galaxy pairs in transverse separation bins of $4$--$6$, $6$--$15$, and $15$--$25\,\hinvMpc$,
subtracted a control built from the convergence map around individual galaxies to form a residual map, and constructed statistics based on the residual map.
The same procedure has been applied to a
CMASS-like mock built from the BigMDPL $N$-body simulation, which provides a $\Lambda$CDM reference model for predictions. 

In the residual map, which shows the difference between the matter distribution around true galaxy pairs and that around control pairs (from superposition of single galaxy stacks), a quadrupole pattern is revealed, with excess around the pair connection line and deficit around the perpendicular bisector. It is the first time that this pattern has been discovered in observation. Such a pattern is consistent with the expectation from the simulation model. It is a manifestation of the gravitational interaction between galaxy or halo pairs. Tidal interaction tends to make the matter distribution stretched along the pair connection line and squashed along the perpendicular bisector direction. For models without dark matter, such as the Modified Newtonian dynamics (MOND; \citealt{Milgrom1983}), the gravitational effect comes from the concentrated distribution of baryonic matter in galaxies, which would make the observed quadrupole pattern hard to produce. 

The quadrupole pattern is not seen in \citet{deGraaff2019} with a similar CMB lensing stacking analysis. This may be related to their construction of the control pair $\kappa$ map. Instead of superposition of the $\kappa$ profile around single galaxies, they derive their control map effectively from averaging the pair $\kappa$ over a certain range along the direction perpendicular to the pair connection line. Such a control map tends to suppress the relatively weak quadrupole pattern. The weak lensing analysis of matter distribution around galaxy pairs in \citet{EppsHudson2017} does not reveal the quadrupole pattern, either. It may be buried in the noise or systematics in the weak lensing analysis. As a comparison, their filament detection results are not seen in \citet{Xia2020}, with an increased sample of source galaxies for weak lensing.

We construct a band profile from the residual map by taking the difference between the average profiles near the pair connection line and that offset from the connection line in the perpendicular direction. The band profile serves to quantify the appearance of filaments. In the band profiles of the $r_\perp=4$--$6$ and $6$--$15\,\hinvMpc$ bins, there appears to be a coherent excess between the two galaxies, signaling the filament, while in the $15$--$25\,\hinvMpc$ bin the observed excess is concentrated near the galaxy positions. The amplitude of the excess broadly agrees with the simulation model. When the jackknife covariance is accounted for, the bridge excess is $2.4\sigma$ in the $6$--$15\,\hinvMpc$ bin and consistent with zero in the other two. If we reduce the smoothing from 8 arcmin (FWHM) to 4 arcmin, the detection of the excess for $r_\perp=4$--$6$ pairs increases to 2.6$\sigma$.

%\textbf{Prospects.} 
The CMB lensing noise appears to dominate the error budget in our analysis.
Increasing the number of pairs
should reduce the statistical error until correlations in the convergence
map become limiting. We find that this limit is already close. The
$r_\perp=6$--$15\,\hinvMpc$ bin contains $2.5$ times as many pairs as the
$9$--$11\,\hinvMpc$ bin of Section~\ref{sec:robust}, but its bridge error is
smaller by only a factor of $1.3$, where independent pairs would give $1.6$.
A larger gain requires a less noisy convergence map (e.g., ACT DR6; \citealt{ACT2024}), together with a large footprint overlap with galaxy surveys. A joint galaxy-shear and CMB-lensing analysis of matter distribution around galaxy pairs would provide useful consistency check and improve the signal-to-noise ratio. Combining the lensing study of matter distribution around galaxy pairs with the thermal Sunyaev--Zel'dovich study \citep[e.g.,][]{deGraaff2019} of the corresponding baryon distribution would help probe the physical processes related to interactions of galaxy pairs.
