We search for the gravitational lensing signal of cosmic-web filaments by
stacking the Planck 2018 CMB lensing convergence map on pairs of BOSS CMASS
galaxies at $z\simeq0.55$. We stack pairs without rescaling them to a common
separation, using a fixed comoving grid in transverse-separation bins centered
at $5$, $10$, and $20\,\hinvMpc$. We subtract a parameter-free control made by
placing the observed single-galaxy stack at the two galaxy positions, then
measure the residual as the difference between a central band along the pair
axis and two bracketing bands. We combine the North and South Galactic caps
and estimate uncertainties and profile covariances with a $287$-region spatial
jackknife. Applying the same estimator to a CMASS-like mock built from the
BigMDPL simulation predicts bridge convergences of
$(6.3,\,4.9,\,2.2)\times10^{-4}$ as separation increases. The observed values
are $(5.4\pm9.1,\,11.9\pm5.1,\,1.2\pm2.2)\times10^{-4}$, corresponding to
$0.6\sigma$, $2.3\sigma$, and $0.6\sigma$. Thus no individual bin provides a
significant detection, and the measurements are consistent with both zero
bridge signal and the mock prediction. The uncertainty is dominated by the
Planck reconstruction noise, so detecting the predicted separation-dependent
signal requires a lower-noise convergence map.
