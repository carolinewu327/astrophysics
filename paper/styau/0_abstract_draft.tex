We search for the gravitational lensing signal of cosmic-web filaments by
stacking the Planck 2018 CMB lensing convergence map on pairs of BOSS CMASS
galaxies at $z\simeq0.55$. Unlike previous pair-stacking analyses, we do not
rescale pairs to a common separation: pairs are stacked on a fixed comoving
grid in three transverse-separation bins centred at $5$, $10$, and
$20\,\hinvMpc$, so that the residual signal can be followed as a function
of separation. The contribution of the two haloes is removed with a control
built from the observed single-galaxy stack placed at the two pair
positions, with no fitted parameters, and the filament is measured as the
difference between a central band along the pair axis and the bands
bracketing it. The North and South Galactic caps are stacked as one joint
sample and uncertainties come from a $287$-region spatial jackknife with the
full covariance. The same pipeline is applied to a CMASS-like mock built
from the BigMDPL simulation, which predicts a bridge convergence of
$6.3$, $4.9$, and $2.2\times10^{-4}$ at the three separations,
declining with separation as the fraction of connected pairs falls.
The observed bridge is consistent with zero at $0.6\sigma$, $2.3\sigma$, and
$0.6\sigma$,
with errors that exceed the predicted signal at every separation, so the
data are consistent with the $\Lambda$CDM expectation and cannot yet test
it. The error budget is set by the Planck reconstruction noise, not by the
control. On the mock, a control whose amplitudes are fitted along the pair
axis absorbs the bridge entirely, a caution for pair-stacking analyses
generally. A detection of the predicted signal requires the lower-noise
convergence maps now available from ground-based experiments.
