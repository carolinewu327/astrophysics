\section{Methods}
\label{sec:method}

Every step described here is applied identically to the Planck map around
BOSS pairs and to the simulated convergence map around mock pairs. The only
differences are those forced by the data: the observed stacks carry survey
weights, a sky mask, and a spatial jackknife over the footprint, whereas the
mock stacks are unweighted, periodic, and jackknifed over blocks of the box.

\subsection{Pair selection}
\label{sec:pairs}

For every galaxy we compute the comoving distance $D_{\rm c}=\chi(z)\,h$ from
its spectroscopic redshift. Two galaxies form a pair if their line-of-sight
and transverse separations,
\begin{equation}
  r_{\parallel} = |D_{{\rm c},1}-D_{{\rm c},2}|, \qquad
  r_{\perp} = \theta_{12}\,\frac{D_{{\rm c},1}+D_{{\rm c},2}}{2},
  \label{eq:separations}
\end{equation}
with $\theta_{12}$ the great-circle angle between them, satisfy
$r_{\parallel}\leq r_{\parallel,{\rm max}}$ and
$r_{\perp,{\rm min}}\leq r_{\perp}\leq r_{\perp,{\rm max}}$. Because
$r_{\parallel}$ is computed from redshifts it is a redshift-space quantity
and includes the pairwise peculiar velocity (Section~\ref{sec:intro}). We
use three transverse bins, $4$--$6$, $9$--$11$, and $18$--$22\,\hinvMpc$,
referred to below as the $5$, $10$, and $20\,\hinvMpc$ samples, with
line-of-sight cuts of $r_{\parallel,{\rm max}}=5\,\hinvMpc$ for the
$5\,\hinvMpc$ sample and $10\,\hinvMpc$ for the other two. The cuts were
fixed on the mock before the observed stacks were examined
(Section~\ref{sec:sim_expect}). Each unordered pair is counted once. A
galaxy may belong to several pairs and we apply no isolation criterion; the
resulting correlation between pairs is handled by the spatial jackknife
rather than by down-weighting. The pair is assigned the comoving distance
$D_{\rm mid}=\chi(\bar z)\,h$ at the mean redshift of its members. Random
pairs are selected from the full random catalogues with the same criteria.

\subsection{Stacking in the pair frame}
\label{sec:stacking}

Each pair defines a local Cartesian frame on the sky: the $X$ axis runs
from galaxy 1 to galaxy 2, with the pair ordered so that $X$ increases with
Galactic longitude, and the $Y$ axis is perpendicular to it. Galaxy 1 and
galaxy 2 sit at $X=\mp r_{\perp}/2$, $Y=0$, at their own separation, so
within a bin the halo positions spread over the bin width. We lay down a
$101\times101$ grid of points spanning $\pm50\,\hinvMpc$ in both
directions, a spacing of exactly $1\,\hinvMpc$ with a grid point at the
midpoint. A grid point $(X,Y)$ is mapped to Galactic coordinates in the
small-angle approximation,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta l\cos b_{\rm c} = \frac{X\cos\phi - Y\sin\phi}{D_{\rm mid}}, \qquad
  \Delta b = \frac{X\sin\phi + Y\cos\phi}{D_{\rm mid}},
  \label{eq:grid_to_sky}
\end{equation}
where $(l_{\rm c},b_{\rm c})$ is the pair midpoint and $\phi$ is the
position angle of the pair axis, $\tan\phi=\Delta b/(\Delta l\cos b_{\rm c})$.
Each sky position is assigned the value of the HEALPix pixel that contains
it. At $N_{\rm side}=2048$ the pixel scale of $1.7\,\mathrm{arcmin}$ is
finer than the grid spacing, which is $2.4\,\mathrm{arcmin}$ at $z=0.55$,
so no interpolation is applied. Masked pixels receive zero weight. The rare
pairs whose grid extends past a Galactic pole are dropped.

The stacked map is the weighted mean at each grid point,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\kappa(X,Y) = \frac{\sum_{i} w_{i}\,m_{i}(X,Y)\,\kappa_{i}(X,Y)}
                         {\sum_{i} w_{i}\,m_{i}(X,Y)},
  \label{eq:stack}
\end{equation}
where $i$ runs over pairs, $w_{i}=w_{1}w_{2}$ is the pair weight
(Section~\ref{sec:data_cmass}), and $m_{i}\in\{0,1\}$ is the mask value at
the sampled pixel. The pair frame has a four-fold symmetry: the two
galaxies are statistically interchangeable, and the sky above and below the
axis is equivalent. We therefore replace the value at $(X,Y)$ by the mean of
the values at $(\pm X,\pm Y)$, which averages four measurements of the same
physical quantity and removes any residual asymmetry between the two
members.

Single galaxies and single random points are stacked in the same way on a
$100\times100$ grid of $1\,\hinvMpc$ cells centred on the object, with the
axes aligned with Galactic longitude and latitude and the weight $w$ of
Equation~(\ref{eq:weights}) multiplied by the lensing-efficiency factor
(Section~\ref{sec:data_cmass}). Because a single stack has no preferred
direction it is symmetrised radially: pixels are averaged in annuli about
the physical centre of the grid, which for an even grid lies between the
four central pixels.

\subsection{Joint footprint and random subtraction}
\label{sec:joint}

The North and South caps are accumulated into a single running total, so
Equation~(\ref{eq:stack}) is evaluated once over all objects. This differs
from averaging two per-cap maps: with $73$ per cent of the galaxies in the
North, an equal-weight average would give each Southern galaxy $2.7$ times
the influence of a Northern one and would carry the noisier Southern map
into the combined profile at half weight. % CHECK: percentages from footprint_stats.txt (579,089 / 792,294)
Single galaxies and single randoms are stacked by the same code with the
same weights, and the corrected single map is their difference,
$\hat\kappa_{\rm g}-\hat\kappa_{\rm r}$. The random-point map absorbs the
mean convergence of the footprint and any offset associated with the mask
or the mean field of the reconstruction.

Random pairs are far too numerous to store, of order $10^{9}$ over both
caps, so they are stacked as they are found. They are stacked separately
for each cap and combined with weights proportional to the number of pairs
in each. The random-pair map is subtracted from the galaxy-pair map and is
held fixed in the error analysis; its own noise, estimated from the scatter
between subsamples, is below the level of the galaxy-pair errors.

\subsection{Control for the halo contributions}
\label{sec:control}

The pair stack contains the one-halo and isotropic two-halo contributions of
both members in addition to any anisotropic excess (Section~\ref{sec:intro}).
We model the isotropic part with a control built from the corrected single
stack. Let $P(r)$ be the radial profile of the radially symmetrised
corrected single map, interpolated linearly in radius. The control for a
pair of separation $d$ is the superposition
\begin{equation}
  T(X,Y) = P\!\left(\sqrt{(X-d/2)^{2}+Y^{2}}\right)
         + P\!\left(\sqrt{(X+d/2)^{2}+Y^{2}}\right),
  \label{eq:control}
\end{equation}
evaluated at the bin-centre separation on the $101\times101$ pair grid. It
has unit amplitude and no free parameters. The filament map is
\begin{equation}
  F(X,Y) = \left[\hat\kappa_{\rm gp}(X,Y)-\hat\kappa_{\rm rp}(X,Y)\right]
           - T(X,Y),
  \label{eq:filament}
\end{equation}
where gp and rp denote the galaxy-pair and random-pair stacks.

The control represents two average CMASS galaxies placed at the pair
positions. It therefore removes the one-halo terms and the two-halo term of
a galaxy with an average environment. It does not know that galaxies
selected to have a close companion sit in denser regions and in somewhat
more massive haloes, nor that their environment is elongated along the pair
axis. Both effects survive in $F$: the first as an isotropic residual around
each halo, the second as the anisotropic pattern we are looking for. The
mock, where the same construction is applied to a catalogue with known halo
and filament content, calibrates what $F$ contains
(Section~\ref{sec:sim_expect}). We deliberately do not fit the amplitude of
$T$ to the observed pair stack: because the pair axis is aligned with the
surrounding large-scale structure on both sides of the pair, any fit region
along the axis is correlated with the bridge, and a fitted control absorbs
part of it. Section~\ref{sec:robust} quantifies this on the mock.

\subsection{Band statistic}
\label{sec:bands}

Rather than interpret the two-dimensional map pixel by pixel, we compress
$F$ into a profile along the pair axis. For each column $X$ we take the
mean of $F$ in a central band $|Y|\leq1.5\,\hinvMpc$, where a filament
would lie, and in an off-centre band $1.5\leq|Y|\leq10.5\,\hinvMpc$ that
brackets it, and form their difference,
\begin{equation}
  B(X) = \langle F(X,Y)\rangle_{|Y|\leq1.5}
       - \langle F(X,Y)\rangle_{1.5\leq|Y|\leq10.5}.
  \label{eq:band}
\end{equation}
The band widths are fixed in comoving units at every separation, so all
three samples are measured with the same ruler. Rows straddling a band
boundary are given fractional weights equal to the fraction of the row that
lies inside the band, so the statistic does not depend on where pixel edges
fall; on the $101$ grid the bands are three and eighteen whole rows. The
difference removes anything that varies slowly with $Y$, including a
residual offset shared by both bands, and reduces the map to one number per
position along the axis. The bridge excess is the mean of $B(X)$ over the
window $|X|\leq0.35\,d$ between the galaxies, which stops $0.15\,d$ short of
each halo centre. For covariance-based fits the profile is further compressed
into a vector of the central and off-centre band means in eleven bins of
$4\,\hinvMpc$ along $X\geq0$ out to $40\,\hinvMpc$, $22$ numbers in all; the
bin width matches the smoothing scale so adjacent bins are only weakly
correlated.

After smoothing, the filament is at least $3.3\,\hinvMpc$ wide at half
maximum, so part of it falls in the off-centre band and the difference in
Equation~(\ref{eq:band}) under-reports the bridge amplitude. The mock
provides the transfer factor: the same statistic is applied to the
simulated filament map, and all comparisons between data and prediction are
made in the compressed statistic rather than in map amplitude.

\subsection{Errors and covariance}
\label{sec:errors}

Errors on the observed maps come from a delete-one spatial jackknife. The
footprint is tessellated with HEALPix cells of $N_{\rm side}=10$, area
$34.4\,\mathrm{deg}^{2}$, traced by a seeded 10 per cent subsample of the
randoms. Cells with at least $70$ per cent of the mean occupancy become
region cores. Partially covered cells at the survey edge are not dropped:
each is merged into the least-populated of its four nearest cores,
processing the most populated edge cells first, so every object belongs to
a region and the regions stay close to equal in size. The joint footprint
yields $K=287$ regions with an RMS size scatter of $11$ per cent. % CHECK: 287 and 11% quoted in CLAUDE.md / docstrings; regions file not in the repo
No region straddles the two caps. Galaxies and random points are assigned to
regions by their own positions and pairs by their midpoints.

During stacking we accumulate $\sum w\kappa$ and $\sum w$ separately for
each region, so the leave-one-out map for region $k$ is obtained by
subtracting that region's totals from the full sums rather than by
re-stacking. All $287$ realisations therefore cost about as much as one
stack. Galaxies and randoms share the tessellation, so each realisation is a
valid version of the corrected map and the error applies to the subtracted
quantity. For any statistic $x$ (a pixel, a band value, or the compressed
vector), the jackknife variance and covariance are
\begin{equation}
  \sigma^{2}=\frac{K-1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(x_{k}-\bar x)^{2}, \qquad
  \mathsf{C}=\frac{K-1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}})
                                          (\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}})^{\mathsf T},
  \label{eq:jackknife}
\end{equation}
with $\bar x$ the mean of the leave-one-out values. The full chain of
Equations~(\ref{eq:control})--(\ref{eq:band}) is rebuilt inside every
realisation: the control is regenerated from the leave-one-out single stack
and subtracted from the leave-one-out pair stack, so the uncertainty of the
control propagates into the bridge error. The random-pair map is the one
quantity held fixed.

The covariance matters because the $8$-arcmin beam spans more than three
grid pixels. Treating pixels or $1\,\hinvMpc$ bins as independent
understates the error on a band mean by a factor of $2.2$ to $2.6$ on the
joint stack. % CHECK: CLAUDE.md, fit_sim_template.py docstring
All quoted errors on band quantities are jackknife errors on the quantity
itself, and all fits use $\mathsf{C}$. With $K=287$ realisations and a
$22$-element vector the Hartlap correction to $\mathsf{C}^{-1}$ is
$4$ per cent; we report it but do not apply it. % CHECK: fit_band_covariance.py computes, does not apply

Where we fit the amplitude $A$ of a fixed template $\mathbf{s}$ to an
observed vector $\mathbf{d}$ we use
\begin{equation}
  \hat A=\frac{\mathbf{s}^{\mathsf T}\mathsf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{d}}
              {\mathbf{s}^{\mathsf T}\mathsf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{s}}, \qquad
  \sigma_{A}=\left(\mathbf{s}^{\mathsf T}\mathsf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{s}\right)^{-1/2},
  \label{eq:amplitude}
\end{equation}
and test for an additive constant by nesting a model with a free offset.

\subsection{The simulated pipeline}
\label{sec:sim_pipeline}

The mock analysis repeats Sections~\ref{sec:stacking}--\ref{sec:bands} on
the simulated convergence map of Section~\ref{sec:sim}. Mock pairs are
selected in redshift space with the same $r_{\perp}$ bins and
$r_{\parallel}$ cuts as the data. % CHECK: the mock 20 hMpc bin is 19-21 in run_sim_sensitivity.py, the data bin is 18-22
Mock pairs and mock single galaxies are unweighted. The single stack uses
every mock galaxy, so the control is built exactly as for the data. Errors
come from a delete-one jackknife over a $5\times5$ tiling of the box face,
$500\,\hinvMpc$ blocks assigned by pair midpoint, with the same
$(K-1)/K$ estimator; the control is held fixed in the mock jackknife because
the single stack over $10^{6}$ haloes is effectively noise-free.

Three further mock products are used. First, a line-of-sight sweep repeats
the pair selection with $r_{\parallel,{\rm max}}=5$, $10$, $20$, and
$30\,\hinvMpc$ and records, for each cut, the completeness (the fraction of
truly associated pairs, real-space $|r_{\parallel}|\leq20\,\hinvMpc$, that
survive the redshift-space cut), the interloper fraction (the fraction of
selected pairs whose real-space separation exceeds $20\,\hinvMpc$), and the
jackknife signal-to-noise of the bridge excess. The production cuts were
chosen before the observed stacks were examined as the cuts with the highest
mock signal-to-noise among those with completeness above $90$ per cent and
interloper fraction below $10$ per cent. Second, a non-physical-pair test
stacks mock pairs with real-space line-of-sight separations of $20$--$40$,
$40$--$60$, $60$--$90$, and $90$--$120\,\hinvMpc$, which should show no
bridge, and checks that the control leaves no residual there. Third, a
sensitivity grid varies the smoothing, the $r_{\parallel}$ cut, the bin
half-width, and the halo mass of the tracer sample.

\section{Expected signal from the simulation}
\label{sec:sim_expect}

Figure~\ref{fig:sim_pair_vs_control} shows the mock pair stack at
$r_{\perp}=10\,\hinvMpc$ beside its control. On a common colour scale the
two are nearly indistinguishable: the filament is a small residual between
two large and similar maps, which is why the construction of the control
matters. Subtracting the control leaves the pattern shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:sim_residual_maps} at all three separations: a positive
ridge along the pair axis between the galaxies and a deficit above and
below it. The ridge is the bridge; the deficit arises because the matter
around a paired halo is concentrated toward the axis, so an isotropic
control over-predicts the convergence perpendicular to it. The quadrupole is
therefore the expected signature, not an artefact, and the band difference
of Equation~(\ref{eq:band}) is designed to measure it.

\subsection{Comparison with the simulation and with previous work}
\label{sec:compare}

Three comparisons are meaningful, and each must be made in the same
compressed statistic with the same bands, window, and smoothing applied to
both maps.

The first is the bridge amplitude itself, the last column of
Table~\ref{tab:bridge}. The observed errors exceed the mock prediction at
every separation, so the data cannot distinguish the $\Lambda$CDM bridge
from zero; the useful statement is the upper limit. Converting the
$95$ per cent upper limit on the bridge excess to a surface density through
$\Sigma_{\rm crit}$ and to a linear mass density over the bridge length
gives % TODO: compute; compare with Epps & Hudson (2017) filament mass and de Graaff et al. (2019) overdensity 5.5 +/- 2.9

The second is the halo residual. The observed filament map peaks at the
galaxy positions at about $2\times10^{-3}$, against $0.9\times10^{-3}$ in
the mock. % CHECK: fit_sim_template.py docstring, flagged as an open item
Paired CMASS galaxies are thus more strongly biased relative to the average
CMASS galaxy than paired mock galaxies are relative to the average mock
galaxy. A central-only HOD with a soft mass threshold produces a weaker
mass selection in pairs than the real sample, which includes satellites and
a sharper stellar-mass cut. Independently, the single-galaxy stack itself
runs below the matched mock prediction at large radius. % CHECK: fit_sim_template.py docstring; compare_boss_mock_profile.py retracted earlier sigma values
Both point to the mock two-halo term being too extended relative to CMASS,
and neither affects the bridge statistic directly because the band
difference removes the isotropic part, but they set the size of the
isotropic residual that the anisotropic signal must be separated from.
% TODO: band comparison of corrected single profiles at 10-20, 15-25, 20-40 hMpc (combine_jackknife DEFAULT_BANDS) with jackknife errors

The third is the comparison with \citet{deGraaff2019}, who measured
$\bar\kappa=(5.8\pm3.1)\times10^{-4}$ in the bridge region of pairs pooled
over $6$--$14\,\hinvMpc$. Their statistic differs from ours in four ways:
pairs are rescaled to unit separation before stacking, the bridge region is
defined on the rescaled grid, the halo model is fitted to the pair stack in
a perpendicular cone, and the map is smoothed to $10$ arcmin. Their central
value is comparable to our $10\,\hinvMpc$ excess, and their error is
smaller by a factor that follows partly from pooling about $2.2$ times as
many pairs, worth a factor $1.5$, and partly from averaging over a larger
bridge area. % CHECK: recompute the ratio once the observed error is filled in

\paragraph{Random-pair sample.} Replacing the full random catalogue by a

$5\times10^{-4}$, comparable to the signal, which is why the full randoms

\paragraph{Fitted control.} % UNVERIFIED: describes code not in the repo
As an alternative to the unit-amplitude control we also split $P(r)$ at
$r_{\rm c}=3\,\hinvMpc$ into a core and a tail and fitted the two amplitudes
to the region outside the pair along the axis. On the mock this control
drives the bridge excess to zero at all three separations, whereas the
unfitted control recovers the known bridge. The fitted control therefore
absorbs the signal through the alignment of the pair axis with the
surrounding structure, and we do not use it for the headline measurement.
